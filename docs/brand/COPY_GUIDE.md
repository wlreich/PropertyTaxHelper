# ParcelSavvy copy guide

## Voice

**Clear:** ordinary words, short explanations, visible calculations. **Calm:** acknowledge concern without manufacturing fear or urgency. **Curious:** invite investigation; do not jump to a conclusion. **Candid:** state what the data establishes and what remains unknown. **Empowering:** offer evidence and a practical next step without pressure.

Product: ParcelSavvy. Tagline: “Know your property. Understand your assessment.” Positioning: independent assessment explanation and transparency. We are not TCAD, a law firm, a licensed appraisal opinion, or a service guaranteeing protest outcomes. The broader product vision is not a claim that a future feature is available today.

## Writing principles

Use sentence case, active voice, specific nouns and short labels. Define technical terms where they first become useful. Prefer “review,” “evidence,” “context,” “appraised value,” “assessment,” and “comparable property.” Explain a term before explaining its significance. Keep material limitations visible; a hover explanation cannot carry a qualification necessary to interpret the number.

Distinguish market value, appraised value, assessed value and the actual tax bill. Verify jurisdiction-specific definitions before adding legal guidance. Do not call a difference an error or label a property overassessed unless supporting evidence warrants it. Never infer trends from one imported year. Do not call land segments homes, bedrooms or living area. Preserve units, including decimal acreage, and distinguish unknown from zero.

## Use / avoid

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| “Your assessed value increased 12%. Here is how it compares with similar properties nearby.” (only with verified years/calculation) | “The appraisal district raised your taxes again. Fight back now!” |
| “These properties share key characteristics. Review the differences before choosing comparables.” | “Perfect comps to guarantee a reduction.” |
| “This result may be worth a closer look.” | “You are definitely overpaying.” |
| “The available records do not establish whether this value is too high.” | “Guaranteed savings.” |
| “Source: TCAD, 2026 certified release.” | “Current value” without a source date |
| “Not reported” / “Under review” when applicable | “$0” substituted for missing or withheld data |

## Screen guidance

**Search:** show a specific label (“Property address or property ID”), an example/partial-address hint, and one primary action (“Search property”). A full address is not required. Explain the scope of optional filters; “Show all parcels” includes identified parkland but does not bypass confidentiality restrictions.

**Results:** address first, concise metadata, value with a clear label, source year/stage and available export date. Use neutral labels such as “Parkland” only when supported by source classification. Preserve unknown types instead of guessing residential status.

**Comparisons:** explain selection criteria, source year, sample size, material differences and adjustments. Avoid “perfect comparable,” “proof,” and implied guaranteed reductions. A nearby parcel is not automatically comparable. Show when sale data is unavailable.

**Explanations and education:** define a term first, then why it matters, with a verifiable source. Example: “Market value: TCAD’s estimate of what the property would sell for as of January 1 of the source year. It is not your tax bill.” Support keyboard and touch as well as hover. Time-sensitive tax/legal guidance must cite current authoritative sources and distinguish general explanation from individualized advice.

**Warnings and uncertainty:** name the limitation and a practical next step. “More data needed” is appropriate when a conclusion cannot be supported; do not show alarming red simply because a value is higher. “Worth a closer look” requires evidence, not a marketing trigger.

**Empty states:** “No matching addresses found. Try a street name or check the spelling.” Offer filter guidance when relevant. Do not imply the property does not exist just because it is absent from this release.

**Errors:** explain the interruption without credentials, raw errors or blame. “Property search is temporarily unavailable. Please try again in a few minutes.” Preserve the query and offer official TCAD search. Never render an outage as an empty successful search.

**Loading:** describe the actual action (“Finding your property…”); do not show invented progress percentages.

**Calls to action:** “Search property,” “View details,” “Review the evidence,” “Compare properties” (when available). Avoid “Fight back,” “Claim your savings,” “Guaranteed reduction,” and pressure-driven countdowns.

## Advertising and affiliates

Keep search and evidence primary. Ads and commercial recommendations must be clearly labeled and visually separated from source records and conclusions. Use “Advertisement” or “Sponsored” for paid placements; label affiliate relationships next to the relevant recommendation, for example “We may earn a commission if you use this link.” Never imply endorsement by TCAD, hide a commercial relationship in a tooltip, manufacture favorable evidence to encourage a click, or block basic explanations with intrusive upsells. Add no ad placements or pricing claims until the relevant feature is approved.

## Enforcement and review

The brand checker scans active UI for a short prohibited-phrase list and the old product name. Documentation examples are deliberately excluded. Automated detection cannot prove claims are supported: reviewers must verify data, calculation, source, date and limitations. Document exceptional quoted educational material rather than weakening the rule globally.
